**Source:** `art/originals/IMG_4228.jpeg` – `IMG_4239.jpeg` (12 photos)
**Cataloged:** 2026-07-08 (Sonnet subagent read all 12 photos; anchors IMG_4228/IMG_4232 confirmed against handoff notes)

**Important caveat up front:** The photos contain more distinct illustrated panels (10) than the box's obvious exterior faces (front, back, left/right long side, lid top, lid interior, bottom = 6). Combined with the geometry — front paper compartment + **two rear drawers** — the extra panels almost certainly belong to the drawer fronts/sides. The cataloger could not physically handle the box, so for several images the *face label* is a best inference from content and staging, not a confirmed placement; confidence is flagged on each. Several photos also show the same two background objects (a yellow "Terrible Writers & Artists" panel and a black-dot patterned panel) blurred in frame even when they aren't the subject — either the same box mid-rotation or a second nearby prop; worth a quick real-world check before final placement.

## IMG_4231 — Lid top (exterior) — ⚠️ lid-placement flag

**Face/view:** Lid, exterior/top surface (photographed while the lid is lifted open by hand — this is the outward-facing surface seen when the box is closed).

**Artwork content:** A continuous panorama: a floral border strip (small multicolor flowers on a dotted vine), an orange tabby cat playing with a hose/showerhead (water rendered as light-blue swirls), the hose curling into a long lavender-to-magenta scaled dragon/sea-serpent tail (dense colored-pencil scale texture), culminating in a stylized reptile/fish head rendered as a cyan-green **stained-glass mosaic** face with yellow cat-eye pupils.

**Technique notes:** Almost entirely colored-pencil tonal rendering with heavy layered shading (the scaled dragon body especially) — very little of this is clean linework; will likely need to be treated as raster/halftone rather than vector line for laser engraving.

**Photo quality issues:** Shot at an angle while hand-held open, causing perspective foreshortening (proportions will need correcting before mapping to a flat unwrapped face); a glare band from tape reflection cuts across the mid-right scales; fingers partially occlude the bottom-left corner.

**Lid observation:** ⚠️ This is a single continuous artwork meant to be read as one panorama across the lid's top surface. Combined with IMG_4229's *different* edge trim (zigzag) vs IMG_4228's edge trim (floral) vs the mosaic-tile design revealed inside the lid (IMG_4232), it appears the cardboard lid was decorated on **all its faces/edges independently** — consistent with a flip lid where every surface becomes visible at some point. A sliding lid, by contrast, keeps one edge permanently retracted into the box and never exposes its "underside" the same way — so this panorama and the tile interior may need to be redesigned or reassigned rather than mapped 1:1 onto the wooden sliding lid.

## Observations Relevant to the Sliding-Lid vs. Flip-Lid Question

1. **The lid carries at least two, possibly three, distinct artworks on different surfaces**: a floral trim strip (visible along the top edge in IMG_4228), a dark chevron/zigzag trim strip (visible along the top edge in IMG_4229 — a *different* pattern on what appears to be a different edge of the same lid), a continuous figurative panorama on the top exterior (IMG_4231: cat/hose/dragon/mosaic-reptile), and an abstract checkerboard mosaic on the interior underside (IMG_4232). A flip lid naturally exposes all of these at different points (closed top, open underside, and edges glimpsed in between); a sliding lid keeps the underside permanently hidden against the box body and only ever shows its top surface plus the edge that overhangs when retracted — so **the interior mosaic (IMG_4232) and the lid-edge trims likely have no equivalent surface on a sliding-lid design** and will need a placement decision (drop it, move it inside the box body, or repurpose it as visible-when-open contrast art).
2. **IMG_4231's panorama is a single continuous composition** (floral → cat → dragon → mosaic face) rather than a repeatable/tileable pattern, so it can't simply be cropped to fit a shorter sliding-lid footprint without deciding which portion to keep.
3. Given the wooden box's sliding lid likely has a smaller "always visible" surface than the cardboard flip lid's total surface area (top + underside + edges combined), **expect to lose or relocate roughly half of the lid-related artwork** cataloged here (IMG_4231 and the IMG_4232 mosaic) — flagging this now so it's an explicit decision rather than a default loss during the vector-tracing pass.
