How to play

The telephone game, played with pictures

Fax Machine is the telephone game played on paper. A phrase becomes a drawing, the drawing becomes a new phrase, and the reveal at the end shows how far each one drifted. You need at least three players, and there is no upper limit.

    Repeat until every page is full, then reveal

    After step 8, go back to step 4 with the stack you are handed: draw the words on top, fold them back, pass, and so on. Each page gets one phrase and one drawing, so a stack of four pages makes eight hops. When all the pages are full, unfold each stack and read it from page 1, one step at a time. Always pass in the same direction. Each player uses four pages however many people play; the game has worked with twenty.

        Cut files

        Download the cut files

        1. Measure your plywood

        Plywood sold as 1/8 inch or 3 mm is often anywhere from 2.8 to 3.5 mm. The finger joints only fit if the files match your sheet, so measure it with calipers in three or four spots away from the edges, average the readings, and pick the closest thickness below. Calipers are fine for the thickness; for the kerf, trust the coupon's finger-joint pairs, since telling a 0.18 mm kerf from 0.20 mm needs calipers good to 0.01 mm.

        2. Cut the test coupon first

        The coupon is a small test piece cut from the same sheet as the box. It shows how tight the finger joints come out and how firmly the magnets press in, so you can pick the right files before cutting the whole box. It costs a few cents of wood and can save a sheet.

        Laser starting points

        Published settings for about 3 mm plywood. They are only starting points: every machine, lens and batch of plywood differs, so test on your coupon first and adjust. Each machine uses its own speed and power scale, so do not compare rows.

        Sources checked Oct 2026.

        Color code

        • Blue is cut. Hairline vector lines that go through the sheet.
        • Red is engrave. Raster or score the surface; never cut through.
        • Red lines are labels. Part letters and joint numbers, engraved lightly; turn this layer off if you do not want them.

        Order the cut so small inner holes go first and outer outlines last, which most laser software does by default. If your service does not engrave, the box still works plain.

        Maker notes

        How the files are made

        Every part is generated by a parametric Python program built on boxes.py. You change a few numbers and it redraws all the parts, joints and holes to match.

        The two numbers that matter most are the plywood thickness, which sets the depth of every finger and slot, and the kerf (the width of material the laser burns away), which the generator adds back so joints come out snug. Finger play, the clearances around the drawers and lid, and the magnet press fit are separate settings, and the coupon checks each of them.

        Everything is open: the generator, tests, art and these files.
